2 / Model choice and validation

SES: evolving level. Holt: level plus unrestricted local trend. Damped Holt: local trend that fades with horizon. Holt-Winters: level, trend and recurring monthly seasonality. Auto ETS: chooses additive log-scale structure by training AICc. Naive and seasonal naive are benchmark forecasts.

All smoothing models fit log prices (lambda = 0); forecast means are bias-adjusted back to dollars. Naive benchmarks use original prices. Parameters are estimated by the forecast package. Compare every method on the same dollar-scale errors. Model choice is the lowest validation RMSE among the five smoothing methods; benchmarks remain visible, even when stronger.

Accuracy definitions

Error = actual minus forecast. ME measures bias (positive means underprediction); MAE is average absolute dollar error; RMSE penalizes larger misses more; MAPE is average absolute percentage error; MASE divides MAE by the mean absolute seasonal-naive one-step error in the corresponding fitting period. MASE < 1 beats that in-sample scale, not necessarily the held-out seasonal-naive forecast. Coverage80 / Coverage95 are observed interval coverage fractions across only 12 months, not guaranteed probabilities.

Alpha controls the level update; beta controls the trend update; gamma controls the seasonal update; phi damps trend persistence. NA means the parameter is absent. Near-boundary estimates deserve discussion. The same model structure may have different parameters when refit to more data.

  1. What do the history, ACF and STL plots say about trend, seasonality and shocks?
  2. Do residuals resemble white noise? Which visible features or ACF lags support your answer?
  3. Do RMSE, MAE and MAPE agree on the strongest models? What does disagreement imply?
  4. How did the locked choice perform on the test year and against naive benchmarks?
  5. What does the forecast imply for budgeting, and what would make its intervals unreliable?
  6. What extra data might improve forecasts: flock sizes, feed costs, disease outbreaks, supply volumes or inflation?
